# Puerto Bravo: Ley del Asfalto

**Language of this document:** [Español](README.md) · English · [Tiếng Việt](README_vi.md) · [Português do Brasil](README_pt.md)

> *Este README también está en español: [léelo en español](README.md).*

An open-world audio game, in Spanish, English and Vietnamese, and fully accessible. It is played by ear alone: with the keyboard or with an Xbox or PlayStation controller, and with a screen reader (NVDA, JAWS or the Windows voice). You never need to see the screen.

It is an original game. Its city, its story, its characters and its music are its own.

This document has four parts:

1. [For players](#1-for-players): what the game is and what you will find in it.
2. [For developers](#2-for-developers): setting up the environment, testing, building and publishing.
3. [Creating mods](#3-creating-mods): extending the game with your own content.
4. [Sound and music packs](#4-sound-and-music-packs): changing how it sounds.

---

## A note about the sounds

I want to be honest with anyone who has read this far: I am a developer, and sound is not my strong point. For lack of time and know-how, most of the game's sounds are either generated by the program itself or taken from websites that offer royalty-free recordings. I know they are poor, and that in an audio game sound is almost everything: with good sounds, chosen by people who understand audio and know the game, Puerto Bravo would gain a great deal.

That is why I am open to any collaboration with sound packs, whether full packs or just a few sounds. If you would like to help:

- The guide `data\docs\cambiar_sonidos_en.html` (also in Spanish, `cambiar_sonidos_es.html`) explains what each sound is, what hearing it means, how long the original lasts and how to put a pack together. The section [Sound and music packs](#4-sound-and-music-packs) sums it up.
- The recordings must be shareable with the game: your own, or under a free license (CC0 or similar), stating where each one comes from.
- To send it to me, open a [GitHub issue](https://github.com/hxebolax/PuertoBravo/issues) with a download link to the pack (or the ZIP attached, if it fits), or write to me on Mastodon at [@HXeBoLaX](https://comunidad.nvda.es/@HXeBoLaX). If you know Git, a pull request with the pack in its own folder under `data\sonidos` is welcome too.

Any help, however small, will be very much appreciated. Thank you in advance.

## 1. For players

### The story

You choose a character, or create your own with a name, skills and a starting advantage, and you decide whether you are addressed as a man or as a woman. With the first character you are Dani Roca, and you have just been released from prison after serving three years for a robbery you did not commit. You return to your city, Puerto Bravo, which is now controlled by the businessman Octavio Belmonte. From there, do whatever you like: follow the story, earn an honest living, make a name for yourself on the street or spend your days fishing. The game never forces you into anything.

### How to play without sight

Everything that happens has its own sound, placed around you: what sounds to the right is to the right, and what sounds muffled is behind you. That is why you need headphones. You also have many aids, all of them optional:

- A key that tells you where you are: the street, which avenues you are between, the neighborhood and which way you are facing.
- A directional scanner that works like a cane: what is in front, behind and to the sides, and how many meters away the wall is.
- A radar that sweeps through what is nearby, with distance and direction, playing each thing from where it is.
- Spoken GPS, with a sound beacon that marks the way, to any place in any town.
- A map explorer to learn the streets without moving your character.
- Walking on your own and the GPS autopilot: the character or the car follows the route while you listen.
- Assisted driving: the car keeps to its lane; you decide the speed and where to turn.
- Audible traffic lights, footsteps that sound different on each surface and a sound of its own for each type of place.
- Two assists for exploring without pressure: taking no damage and playing without police.

### What's in the world

- Nine maps as standard: the cities of Puerto Bravo, San Lucero and Monteferro; the towns of Villaseca, Cala Brava and Fuentefría; Isla Tortuga, the Plataforma Neptuno and the Mar de Poniente, which you can sail. The included mods add three more towns and an island.
- Over 300 places as standard: houses, shops, banks, repair shops, casinos, gyms, cinemas, hospitals, piers, helipads, tram stops, train stations, airports and monuments, each with its own sound and description. Almost every venue and house can be walked through inside, room by room.
- 75 missions as standard across the main story, the side missions and five playable tutorials, plus jobs that repeat endlessly. The mods add almost seventy more. Some missions are played by ear (following someone by their footsteps, keeping watch, getting in without making a sound, races against rivals), and some decisions change what comes next: four of them open different branches, with ten endings.
- 39 vehicles: cars, motorbikes, a bicycle, trucks, emergency vehicles, boats, a helicopter and a light aircraft. 22 weapons, with silencers and extended magazines.
- Public transport: a tram with stops in every neighborhood of the cities and tracks you cross on foot, trains between towns, buses, taxis and passenger planes, all with real journeys.
- You can sail the sea, and also swim and dive in it.
- Police with five wanted levels, day and night, rain, traffic with traffic lights, and pedestrians.

### What you can do

- An honest life: a job center, trades with promotions (taxi driver, ambulance and bus driver, firefighter, crane operator, courier, hauler, ice cream seller, fisher), courses, volunteering and businesses of your own.
- A life of crime: the story, contracts, car jobs, robberies and turf wars.
- Leisure: races and regattas, a casino, bingo, the lottery, seven and a half, dominoes, the coin guessing game, boxing, a shooting gallery, fishing, treasure hunting, the cinema, arcades and the stock market.
- Houses with garages, a dog that keeps you company and finds things, bodyguards, a gang of your own, neighborhoods to defend, vehicle upgrades at the repair shop, achievements and skills that improve with practice.
- Talking to anyone on the street: each person has their own name, job and personality. You can ask questions, buy them a coffee, make friends or type whatever you want to say. An optional mode has them answer as played by an artificial intelligence: a free local model that needs no internet, Google AI Studio, OpenAI, Anthropic or another compatible service. The game picks the cheapest model by itself.
- Voices in the scenes: every character in the story and in the included mods has their own voice, generated by computer. They call you by the name of the character you are playing and address you as a man or as a woman; with a character you created, the lines that say your name are generated on your computer. Anyone can add their own recordings. They can be turned off in the options.
- Two sound engines: the classic one and a surround, binaural one, with a different echo in each place.
- Without artificial intelligence, a base of over eleven hundred lines in almost seventy topics (`juego/respuestas.py`) answers whatever you type, according to each person's personality.
- A city that never repeats itself: every day is different (sales, market day, a festival, a strike, the holiday rush), and prices, people and traffic change. No two games are alike.
- Realism to your taste, all optional: hunger, thirst, sleep and hygiene; fuel and gas stations; breakdowns, flat tires and servicing; and traffic rules with fines and a points-based license.
- Playing on a server: a world that stays alive with real players, gangs, territories, houses and businesses, and with traffic, people and police that are the same for everyone. Anyone can set up their own: the server comes with the game, with an accessible panel to run it and a step-by-step guide.
- The radio pauses when you get out and carries on where it left off; with a player and headphones you can also hear it on foot, and it plays in the background inside venues.
- Playing with friends, with nobody else's servers: your own game hosts the session, with or without a password, and you give them an address. You hear each other, find each other with the radar and the GPS, write to each other, ride in the same car, race with bets and do deliveries as a team.
- Seven radio stations with original music, a news station that comments on what you do and another one with your own music. Ambient music that changes with the situation.
- An optional mode for over-18s, turned off by default: relationships, nightlife, substances and their consequences, told without explicit scenes.

### Requirements and getting started

- Windows 10 or 11.
- Stereo headphones.
- A screen reader running, such as NVDA or JAWS. If there is none, the game speaks with the Windows voice.

Download the latest version, unzip the folder wherever you like and run `PuertoBravo.exe`. It installs nothing: it is portable. Inside the game, `F1` tells you the keys at any time, and the main menu opens the full manual, which is in `data\docs\manual_en.html` (and in Spanish, `manual_es.html`) and can be navigated by headings. The game uses the language of Windows, Spanish, English, Vietnamese, Brazilian Portuguese or Russian, and you can choose it in Options, Language. The release notes of each version are written in Spanish and in English: with the game in Spanish you read them in Spanish, and with any other language, in English.

If something goes wrong, the game keeps a log of each session in `data\logs`, with no personal data: zip the session's folder and attach it to an [issue](https://github.com/hxebolax/PuertoBravo/issues), or send it on Mastodon to [@HXeBoLaX](https://comunidad.nvda.es/@HXeBoLaX). The manual explains how.

The game updates itself from its own options. You can choose where from: the game's server, GitHub, both, or an address of your own. Your saved games, your options, your music and your mods are left untouched.

## 2. For developers

### Requirements

- Windows 10 or 11.
- Python 3.13 or later. It is developed with Python 3.15.

### First steps

Everything is done with the `.bat` files in the root folder:

```bat
entorno.bat    :: crea el entorno virtual .venv e instala las dependencias
jugar.bat      :: abre el juego desde el código fuente
editor.bat     :: abre el editor de mods
probar.bat     :: ejecuta las pruebas automáticas
compilar.bat   :: genera dist\PuertoBravo, portable y listo para repartir
publicar.bat   :: comprime, firma y publica la versión compilada
```

In order: `entorno.bat` creates the `.venv` virtual environment and installs the dependencies; `jugar.bat` opens the game from the source code; `editor.bat` opens the mod editor; `probar.bat` runs the automated tests; `compilar.bat` builds `dist\PuertoBravo`, portable and ready to share; `publicar.bat` zips, signs and publishes the built version.

Dependencies (`requirements.txt`): pygame-ce, numpy, sounddevice, soundfile, accessible-output2, wxPython, cx_Freeze and pytest. The AI mode needs no package at all: it talks to the services over HTTPS with the standard library (`juego/ia.py`), asks each one for its list of models and picks one from the cheap range.

The repository includes the audio: the default sounds and the four packs in `data/sonidos` (the Envolvente pack is put together with `herramientas/crear_pack_envolvente.py`), the radio music in `data/radio` and the ambient music in `data/musica`. If any default sound is missing, the game generates it again at startup.

### Structure

- `juego/`: the game's code. `app.py` is the loop and the screen stack; `partida.py` assembles the game session from modules by topic (driving, combat, missions, jobs, honest life, conversation, network play, daily randomness...); `sintesis.py` generates the default sounds; `audio.py` is the mixer with positional sound.
- `editor/`: the mod editor, with wxPython.
- `herramientas/`: sound and guide generation, protection and checking of the build, pack assembly, built-in mods and publishing.
- `pruebas/`: automated tests with pytest.
- `data/datos/`: maps, places, missions, weapons, vehicles, characters and the people on the street with their enemies, in JSON.
- `data/mods/`: the built-in mods, in readable JSON.
- `data/docs/`: the documentation shipped with the game, meant only for players.
- `desarrollo/`: technical documentation that is not shipped, such as the format of mod files.

### The online server

The server (`servidor/`) uses only Python's standard library and works without the game, on Windows, Linux, Mac, a NAS or Docker. It does not carry the content: places, weapons, vehicles and missions are given to it by the game of the first person who joins with each version, signed by the publisher, and different versions coexist on the same server. Its options are described in a single place, `servidor/opciones.py`, from which the example file, the validation, the panel's configuration dialog and the guide are all produced. The panel (`panel_servidor/`, `servidor_panel.py`) is a wxPython window that reuses the mod editor's accessibility. In development it is started with `servidor.bat` or `servidor_panel.bat`. The guide for whoever sets it up is `data\docs\servidor.html`. `herramientas\prueba_servidor_real.py` starts a real server as a separate process and connects ten clients to it over the network.

#### Protocol: what was added in 1.7.1

The protocol version does not change (`VERSION_RED = 2`). Everything new is optional: fields added to messages that already existed, and new messages that an older end ignores. The rule is twofold:

- The game says in its greeting what it understands: `hola.sabe`, a list (`enlinea.SABE`: `rol`, `radio`, `pasajeros`, `mis_vehiculos`, `topes`, `encargos`). An older server does not look at it. The new server stores it in `Jugador.sabe` and talks to anyone who does not send it as it would to a 1.7.0 game.
- The server says what it can do in `ok.funciones` (`nucleo.FUNCIONES`, with those same six words added). The game checks it with `Partida.sv_sabe(que)` before using anything new; with an older server, it says so in a sentence and does not send the message.

What travels:

- **Passengers** (the run-over passenger). `estado.e.pj`: the session identifier of the player in whose vehicle you are riding. The server also knows it from the `act` messages with `que` equal to `sube` or `baja`, which 1.7.0 already sent. `Servidor._va_con(o)` returns the driver if they are still at the wheel, in the same town and less than 45 meters away; with that, `_m_atropello` does not accept the run-over, `_m_atraco` does not let them be mugged "on foot", and `_m_ataque` and `_m_explosion` give them the protection of the bodywork. The position record carries `pj` when relevant. In the game, the driver does not report running over whoever is riding inside (`sv["pasajeros"]`, kept up to date with `act`, and `Remoto.pasajero_de`): this also works against an older server.
- **Vehicle radio**. `estado.e.ra`: the short identifier of the station, only if it is a standard one (`Radio.comunes()`), it is playing and you are at the wheel. The server validates it (text of up to 24 letters, numbers or underscores), stores it in `Jugador.radio` and puts it in the record as `ra`. No audio ever travels, nor paths, nor anything from "My music". The game that hears it loads a snippet from its own copy (`Radio.muestra`, in a thread) and loops it from the vehicle (`EnLinea._sv_radio_ajena`). Game option `radio_coches`; server option, `radio_coches`.
- **Roleplay**. `chat.canal` also accepts `ic` (in character), `yo` (action) and `ooc` (out of character). `ic` and `yo` reach `rol_metros`; `ooc`, everyone. To a game that does not know about `rol`, the server sends it through `cerca` or `todos` with a few words in front. The commands `/yo`, `/me`, `/ep`, `/ic`, `/fp`, `/ooc` and `/ficha` are interpreted by the server in any chat message, before the administration ones, so they work from an older game. `rol_oido` (`canal`, `n`) tells the speaker how many people heard them. `ficha` with `texto` sets your own profile (it is stored in the `ficha` column of `cuentas` and comes back in `ok.ficha`); with `a`, it asks for someone else's, and the reply is `ficha` with `de`, `nombre` and `texto`.
- **My vehicles**. `veh_nombre` (`id`, `nombre`), `veh_traer` (`id`, `x`, `y`, `r`) and `veh_guardar` (`id`, `ref`), only for a parked vehicle whose owner is the one asking. The reply is `veh_mio` with `que` equal to `nombre`, `traer`, `garaje` or `no` (with `motivo`). Everyone else receives the usual `veh_libre` and `veh_fuera`, so an older game sees it correctly. The garage is still the game's business: the server returns the record and removes the vehicle from the street; the game puts it into `j.garajes`, charges and saves.
- **Money caps**. `ok.servidor.economia`, the `saldo` message and the reply to `economia` carry `tope`, `acumula`, `horas`, `margen`, `por_minuto`, `encargos_hora` and `encargo_max`; `saldo` also carries `ganado` and `aceptado`. The margin is stored with the account (columns `margen` and `t_margen`) and accumulates for up to `ganancia_acumula` hours.
- **Repeatable jobs**. `mision_ok` with `encargo: 1` and `n` (the pay) for a mission that the server's world does not know: the `id` is the type (`contrato`, `atraco_banco`, `atraco_local`, `asalto_proveedor`, `encargo_coche`, `conquista`, `defensa` or the identifier of a fight). The server acknowledges up to `encargos_hora` per hour and type, with the maximum given by `mundo["encargos"]` (calculated by `enlinea.encargos_para_servidor` from the game's data) or, if the world does not include it, `nucleo.ENCARGOS_DE_SERIE`, and never more than `encargo_max`. The acknowledged pay is valid for thirty seconds (`Jugador.fiado`).
- **Purchases and dropped connections**. In the game, `_sv_vigilar_gasto` sends a `guardar` two and a half seconds after the money goes down (and no more than one every twelve seconds if spending continues). On the server, `Jugador.gasto_pend` adds up what it sees the player spend on their own since the last `guardar`, and `total_guardado` is the character's saved money; on leaving (`_salida`), `_devolver_sin_guardar` returns the smaller of the two gaps to the balance. Neither what the server takes away (`Jugador.descuento`) nor houses already paid for count.
- **Mission group**. The enemies reported to teammates also carry `deportivo`, `punteria` and `tono`.
- **Shared police**. Each officer's row carries a sixth value, their health.
- **World**. `mundo_servidor()` adds `encargos` and `gente.frases` (what the server's characters say, by class). They are not part of the fingerprint.

#### Different versions on the same server (1.7.1)

Principle: two people with different versions of the game must be able to play together, and beyond that, on as equal a footing as possible. A connection is only rejected by version with the `version_minima` option. The protocol stays at version 2; the server adds `equivalencias` and `nominas` to `ok.funciones`. The part of the server that does not depend on the rest is in `servidor/compat.py`.

**What travels from the game to the server and what does not.** The server does not have the game: what it knows about the world is the text of `enlinea.mundo_servidor()`, which the game of whoever joins gives it (`pide_mundo` and `mundo`, compressed) or which it ships with in `servidor/mundo.json`.

- What travels: for each town, its places (identifier, type, name, coordinates, price, income), its neighborhoods, its streets and its traffic cars; for each weapon, its type, damage, range, rate of fire, accuracy, radius and whether it is automatic; for each vehicle, its health, speed, acceleration, braking and whether it flies; for each mission, its title, its pay and whether it repeats; the names and lines of the server's characters, the police table, the most each repeatable job pays (`encargos`) and the fixed contracts with their payrolls (`empleos`: period, bonus and, per job, the shifts and salary of each position).
- What does not travel: sounds, mission texts, mission steps and enemies, shops and prices, items, clothes, food, jobs, the whole honest life except payrolls, each player's options, or anything from their computer. All of that is provided by each player's game.
- The fingerprint (`enlinea.huella_datos`) only covers identifiers of towns, places, neighborhoods, vehicles and missions, and the weapons' figures. Everything else the world includes (`encargos`, `gente`, `policia`, `empleos`) is not part of it: that is why, if the world saved by the server (`mundo_actual.json`) and the one it ships with (`mundo.json`) have the same fingerprint, the one it ships with wins at startup.

**Who provides the world** (`Servidor._admitir_mundo`). It is called when the fingerprint of whoever joins is not the server's, if `comprobar_datos` is `si` or the server has no world.

| Case | What the server does |
|---|---|
| Empty server, signed world | Adopts it. |
| Empty server, unsigned world | With `mundo_distinto = admitir` and `actualizar_mundo` other than `no`, it adopts it marked as `provisional`; it is replaced by the first signed one that arrives, whatever its version, or by an administrator's with `actualizar_mundo = admin`. Otherwise, it rejects it with a clear message. |
| Signed world from a newer version | Adopts it (`actualizar_mundo` set to `firmado` or `admin`), announces it and kicks no one out. |
| Known world (`mundos_conocidos`, or `compat.CATALOGOS`, which are those of the published versions) | Lets the player in without asking for anything. |
| Signed world it did not know, the same age or older | Checks it, remembers it and lets the player in. |
| Unsigned world (mods) | With `mundo_distinto = admitir`, the default, the player gets in: the server asks for their world only to know what their game knows during that session (`Jugador.cat`), and nothing from it is saved. With `rechazar`, or with `versiones = iguales`, the player does not get in. |

**Equivalences.** From each checked world the server keeps a catalog (`compat.catalogo_de`: identifiers of weapons and vehicles; `meta.catalogos`, and `compat.CATALOGOS` for the published versions). `Servidor._cat(j)` gives the catalog of a player's game if their fingerprint is not the server's. With it:

- `Servidor._enviar` passes the messages in `TRADUCIBLES` through `_traducir`: `tiro`, `herido` and `po_disparo` (the weapon), `evento` with `d:<arma>`, `veh_ocupado`, `veh_libre`, `veh_ok` and `veh_mio` (the model), `ok` (the parked vehicles) and `grupo_msg` with a mission's enemies (their weapon). Whatever that game does not know is replaced with the closest thing it does know (`compat.arma_equivalente`: same class, then figures, name and whether it is automatic; `compat.modelo_equivalente`: whether it flies or not, then figures and name). `_repartir_estados` does the same with the vehicle each player is driving.
- Damage is not touched: `_m_ataque` and `_m_explosion` calculate it with the figures from the server's world before translating anything, and the others are told the name of the weapon the server counted, not the one the message says.
- `_m_dar` gives back to the giver a weapon or ammunition that the other player cannot receive.
- If it is the server that does not know something (its world is older than the player's), no equivalence applies: the weapon counts as bare hands, the vehicle as `utilitario` and the town is left out, with a notice to the player. It is the same for everyone.
- On joining, `ok.mundo.distinto` (`faltan` and `sobran`, with names of weapons and vehicles) and an `aviso` with the same content in one sentence (`compat.texto_diferencias`).

**The character.** `cuentas.partida` stores the entire JSON the game sends, including whatever the server does not know (limit: `MAX_LINEA`, 600 KB; if it does not fit, it is not saved and a notice is given). `cuentas.partida_juego` is the newest version of the game that has saved that character.

- On joining with a game that does not know everything the character carries, `compat.adaptar_partida` gives it a copy without the weapons or garage vehicles it does not have (a 1.7.0 game fails when switching weapons if it is holding one it does not know). The database is not touched.
- On saving with a game older than `partida_juego`, `compat.fundir_partida` restores what that game cannot save: the root and `jugador` keys that are missing (the 1.7.0 game discards the ones it does not know when loading), the weapons it does not know and the garage vehicles it does not know. For what it does know, what it says goes.

**Payrolls** (`_m_contrato`, `cuentas.contrato`). The `contrato` message with `que`: `firmar` (`empleo`, `puesto` 0 or 1), `turno`, `cobrar` (`buena`, `mala`), `ascenso` and `dejar`. The server replies with `contrato` (`que`: `firmado`, `turno`, `puesto` or `no`, and `c` with `empleo`, `puesto`, `turnos`, `fin`, `periodo`, `tope`, `turnos_dia`) or `nomina` (`n`, `turnos`, `cupo`, `faltan`). The money in `nomina` is recorded as justified credit (`_enviar`), just like `dinero`. What the server decides: shifts per day (`nomina_turnos_dia`), the payday (`fin`, in server days), the amount (the position's salary in `mundo["empleos"]`, in proportion to the shifts, plus the bonus if the quota is met and the evaluation is good, capped by `nomina_max`) and that a position is only raised after a period with the quota met. The game (`honrado.py`: `_hon_sv_contrato`, `_hon_nomina`, `hon_sv_al_entrar`, `_svm_contrato`, `_svm_nomina`) lives the contract just as it does offline and only leaves the money in the server's hands. `ok.servidor.nominas` and `ok.servidor.trato_prestigio` say whether that server handles payrolls and whether shops treat you according to your prestige; `Partida.sv_honrado(que)` checks it.

**New server options**: `mundo_distinto` (`admitir`), `nominas` (`si`), `nomina_max` (1500), `nomina_turnos_dia` (3) and `trato_prestigio` (`si`).

**When publishing a version that changes weapons or vehicles**, record the catalog of the version being left behind in `compat.CATALOGOS`: `.venv\Scripts\python.exe herramientas\exportar_mundo_servidor.py --catalogo` writes the entry for the current world, ready to paste. That way, new servers know that version without having to ask it for anything.

**Tests against the real previous version**: `pruebas/test_171_versiones.py` extracts the published 1.7.0 code with `git archive` into a temporary folder (`pruebas/banco_versiones.py`) and runs it in separate processes (`pruebas/agente_versiones.py`), never mixed with the current code. If there is no git or that commit is missing, those tests are skipped, saying why.

**Known limits.** A 1.7.0 server translates nothing and does not restore the character: anyone who joins it with a game older than its world does not hear the new weapons (their game ignores them) and does not see the new vehicles parked. New towns have no equivalent: someone who does not have them cannot see whoever is in them. Items, clothes and food are not in the server's world, so it is not checked whether the recipient knows them (the 1.7.0 game already says it cannot receive them). The server's traffic uses the models from its world; an older game puts a `utilitario` wherever it does not know the model.

#### Orderly shutdown, update notice and reconnection (1.7.1)

The protocol version does not change here either. The game adds `reconexion` to `hola.sabe` and the server to `ok.funciones`.

**The server.** `Servidor.detener(motivo, causa, aviso, vuelve, espera, codigo)` is the only way to stop, and it now does things in order:

1. If `causa` is `actualizacion` or `reinicio` and there is an `aviso`, a countdown: to each game that knows `reconexion` it sends `parada` (`causa`, `en` seconds, `vuelve` estimated seconds); to the rest, a normal `aviso` with the same content as text. It is repeated when 60, 30 and 10 seconds remain (`AVISOS_PARADA`). Anyone who joins during the countdown receives it on joining.
2. `cerrando = True` (nobody else gets in: `rechazo` with `codigo: "reiniciando"`) and it sends everyone `cerrado` with `motivo` (text an older game understands), `causa` (`apagado`, `actualizacion`, `reinicio`) and `vuelve`.
3. It waits for each game's last `guardar` with `ya` (`_guardan`), for up to `parada_espera` seconds and never less than two tenths of a second. A game, new or 1.7.0, responds to `cerrado` by saving and saying goodbye, so no other message is needed.
4. It takes each player out through `_salida` (vehicle parked, `_devolver_sin_guardar`, `_guardar_saldo`), records in `meta.buscados` who was wanted by the police, saves everything and closes. It leaves `causa_parada` and `codigo_salida` (75, `CODIGO_VUELVE`, if it stopped in order to come back; 0 otherwise).

How it is requested from outside the process: the file `parar.txt` (`ARCHIVO_ORDEN`) in the data folder, which the server itself checks every second in `_bucle_lento` (`_mirar_orden`, `orden_externa`). Its first word is `parar`, `actualizar` or `reiniciar`, optionally followed by the seconds of notice. `python -m servidor --parar | --actualizar | --reiniciar` writes it and waits for it to be picked up. The console and chat commands `actualizar` and `reiniciar` do the same (`parar_para_volver`). The terminate signal (systemd, Docker) and logging off or shutting down Windows stop with `causa="reinicio"`, with no countdown and waiting three seconds at most; Control+C and closing the window, with `apagado`. The panel does not use `__main__`: with the server running, the server sees the file, and with it stopped, the window checks it (`Motor.orden_pendiente`); if `Motor.causa_parada` is `actualizacion` the window closes, and if it is `reinicio` it starts again.

Other server changes: `rechazo` carries `codigo` (`version` with `minima`, `lleno`, `reiniciando`); `veh_no` carries `por` (`no_esta`, `ocupado`, `lejos`); someone who comes back while their previous session is still around as a fight "ghost" replaces it, inherits `golpeado` and `agredio` and enters without arrival protection.

**The game.** `enlinea.conectar` returns the reason as a `Motivo`, a string with `codigo` and `minima`. `_svm_cerrado` (with a `causa` other than `apagado`, or with no `causa`, which means an older server) and `_svm_desconectado` call `sv_cortado`: it sends one last `guardar` if the connection is still up, records in `sv_espera` what will need to be rebuilt (group, passenger, when the last save was), clears what the server had set up (`_sv_limpiar_mundo`), invalidates the other players' identifiers and opens `reconexion.Espera`. `_svm_expulsado` never waits. The game session stays in memory with `en_servidor = True` and `red = None`.

`reconexion.Espera` retries with the waits in `ESPERAS` (3, 5, 8, 12 and 15 seconds), in a thread, for `cfg["reconectar_minutos"]`. Depending on `codigo_de(motivo)`: `red`, `reiniciando` and `lleno` keep trying; `version` stops trying and offers to update (it leaves `data/config/volver_servidor.json` with the name and the address, and after `--tras-actualizar` the game offers to go back); `certificado` and any other rejection go out to the menu, explaining why. On getting in, it calls `sv_entrar(enlace, vuelta=True)`: it does not load `ok.partida`, it does not cut the money with `ok.saldo` (the server judges that with `_cuadrar_saldo` on receiving the position, as always), it sends the position and, four tenths of a second later, a `guardar` with the live game session. The vehicle the player was in is recovered with `veh_subir` using its number (`sv["reenganche"]`): if the server no longer has it or has it far away, it is registered again with `veh_tomar`; if someone else is driving it, the player is left on foot. The group is rebuilt by names (`sv["regrupo"]`): whoever led it resends `grupo_invitar` as the others come in, and the others answer the invitation from that name automatically, for 180 seconds.

What is not rebuilt: riding as a passenger (you are left on foot), a business robbery in progress, pending invitations and the hourly count of repeatable jobs, which lives in the server's memory.

**The updater.** Each running server of the built game leaves a record in `servidor\en_marcha\<pid>.json` (`servidor/enmarcha.py`): type (`consola` or `panel`), executable, arguments, working folder, data folder, configuration, whether the server is running and the Windows session. `actualizador.servidores_en_marcha()` reads them, deletes those of dead processes and marks those from another session as `servicio`. `menus_actualizar._instalar` asks for confirmation, and `pedir_parada_servidores()` writes `data\actualizacion\servidores_abiertos.json` and leaves `actualizar` in the `parar.txt` of each data folder. The `.bat` waits for the programs to close (up to three minutes), installs and, whether it goes well or not, calls `PuertoBravo.exe --relanzar-servidores <nota>` before opening the game: `relanzar_servidores()` starts each one with its arguments and its folder (the panel with `--arrancar` if its server was running), minimized and without stealing focus, and writes in `actualizacion.log` a line `SERVIDOR relanzado tipo=...` or `SERVIDOR sin_relanzar tipo=... motivo=...`. It only starts the `PuertoBravoServidor.exe` and `PuertoBravoServidorPanel.exe` in the game's own folder. Tests can change the records folder with the `PUERTOBRAVO_EN_MARCHA` environment variable.

Limits that remain: on a server, mission enemies arrive on foot even if their type travels by vehicle, and the shared police always drive a patrol car; from the police table they take the weapon, the health and how many officers there are.

All the game's content and prices are in the JSON files in `data\datos` (the newest, in `vida.json`), and the mod editor has a section for each thing.

### People, enemies, police and cash registers

`data/datos/gente.json` and `juego/gente.py` hold everything that used to be lists written in the code: people's first names, surnames, jobs and lines, personalities, gangs, the catalog of enemy types, the police table by wanted level and the cash register of each type of venue. `gente.configurar(datos)` is called at the end of `mundo.cargar_datos()`; anything missing from the data takes the standard value. What the rest of the code uses:

- `gente.lista(cual)`, `gente.nombres(genero)`, `gente.oficio_para(edad, genero, rng)`, `gente.caracter(id)`.
- `gente.enemigo(grupo)` resolves a group from an `eliminar` step (with `clase`, or with name, weapon and health as always) and `gente.vestir(persona, ficha)` applies it to a `Persona`. `gente.grupo(clase, n, **serie)` writes a group that works even if the type is not in the data.
- `gente.agentes_nivel(nivel)` gives the type of officer for a patrol; `gente.banda(nombre)` and `gente.tipos_de_banda(nombre)`, the gang data.
- `gente.caja_local(tipo)` and, in the game session, `caja_de_local(lugar)`: what is in the register today and who guards it.
- `Partida.llegar_en_vehiculo(modelo, personas)` brings a group in a vehicle, with the same driving as the patrols (`ia == "enemigos"`), and `bajar_enemigos(v)` gets them out.

The repair shop upgrades (`juego/sistemas.py`) also come from the data: each one states its `efecto`, from the list `sistemas.EFECTOS`, its levels and how much each level gives.

### My vehicles and the chauffeur

`juego/misvehiculos.py` (the game session's `MisVehiculos` mixin, and the phone menus):

- A player's vehicle carries `v.mio = True` and `v.mio_n` (order of age). `Partida.hacer_mio(v)` marks it: it is called by buying, the first repair shop upgrade, taking it out of the garage, having it brought to you and naming it. `_poblar` does not remove the marked ones.
- The ones in the current town are live vehicles; those in other towns wait as data in `Partida.mis_lejos` (`mis_al_cambiar_mapa`, `_reponer_mis_vehiculos`). In the saved game they go in `mis_vehiculos`, with `n` and, for the last one used, `ultimo`; the one you are driving, in `veh` with `mio`. The format number does not change: an older version ignores the new keys, and an older saved game loads without them (`mis_cargar`).
- `ultimo_veh` and `veh_fuera` are still "the last one you used", whether yours or not; if it is yours, it is not duplicated in `veh_fuera`.
- The limit (`vehiculos_propios.tope_calle` in `vida.json`) is applied by `_tope_mis_vehiculos`.
- On a server nothing is marked or saved (`hacer_mio` returns `False`): the vehicles on the street belong to the server. The server adds `mis_vehiculos` to its join reply (the identifiers of the ones parked under the account's name) and accepts `nombre` in `veh_dejar`; an older game or server ignores both. From the list, a parked vehicle can be renamed, brought over and sent to the garage (`veh_nombre`, `veh_traer`, `veh_guardar`; see the protocol, in The online server). The game keeps track in `sv["mios"]` and `sv_mis_vehiculos()`.
- Missions: `mision_vehiculo(m)` and `mision_vehiculo_txt(m)` say what happens to the vehicle; with `vehiculo_libre`, the `robar` step accepts the player's vehicle (`ms["veh_tuyo"]`) and `entregar` does not take it away.
- Chauffeur: `Partida.chofer` is `None` or a dictionary with `estado` (`espera`, `viene`, `aqui`, `contigo`). The vehicle comes with `ia == "chofer"` (`_ia_chofer`); with you inside, `chofer_conduce()` makes `piloto_puesto()` and `modo_asistido()` apply even if the player has neither the autopilot nor assisted driving, and `conducir` ignores the pedals. The gang's vehicle carries `tag == "banda"`; the one from your garage carries no mark and is yours. It is not saved with the game.

`mundo_servidor()` also tells the server the names of its characters (`gente`) and the weapons of its officers by level (`policia`). These are two extra keys: an older server does not look at them, and a new one uses its own if they are missing. The world fingerprint does not depend on them, but it does depend on the weapons and the vehicles: when adding either, `servidor/mundo.json` must be regenerated with `herramientas/exportar_mundo_servidor.py`.

### The street: self-defense, patrols, surrender, helicopter and neighborhood wars

- `juego/calle.py` (the game session's `Calle` mixin) gathers what happens on the street without the player looking for it. The numbers and lines come from `vida.json`, section `calle`; the player's options, from `config.py` (`defensa_propia`, `frases_reacciones`, `charla_peatones`, `soltar_municion`, `soltar_armas`, `patrullas_calle`, `ambulancias`, `helicoptero_policia`).
- Self-defense: an enemy who attacks without having been touched is marked (`p.agresor`, in `marcar_agresor`, which is called from `actualizar_personas` at the moment of the attack). While there is a living aggressor nearby, `_ruido_disparo` does not record a crime, and neither does taking them down. `agente_auxilia` sends an officer after them; `herir_por_otro` is the casualty that does not count against the player. It does not apply to mission or war `tag` values.
- Voices by gender: `calle.genero_de(p)` (the profile if there is one; if not, the tone of voice) and `Calle.voz_persona`. `charla.ficha` gives the gender according to that same tone. `Calle.asustar` is the only place through which a pedestrian starts fleeing.
- Roaming patrols are vehicles with `ia == "policia"` and `ronda = True`; with zero stars they are moved by `patrulla_sin_busqueda`, and when you are wanted, by the usual code. The ones that respond to a fight carry `auxilio` (the moment they give up).
- The ambulance is a vehicle with `ia == "ambulancia"` and `tag == "ambulancia"`; its state is in `Partida.ambulancia`.
- Surrendering: `Calle.rendirse` sets `Partida.rendido`; `actualizar_personas` sends the officers to `agente_detiene`, and `detener(rendicion=True)` applies the penalty from `_pena_rendicion`.
- The helicopter is a vehicle with `ia == "heli_policia"` and `volando = True`, which `actualizar_vehiculos` does not move: it is driven by `Calle._helicoptero`. Everything that goes through vehicles to crash, brake or get in skips the ones with `volando`. `danar_vehiculo` diverts it to `heli_danado`.
- `juego/guerra.py` (the `Guerra` mixin) is the street neighborhood war mode (`guerra_calle`, off by default; section `guerra_barrios`). `banda_guerra` gives each city neighborhood a stable gang using the game's seed; `miembro_de_barrio` decides who comes out onto the street in `_poblar`; the state of the war in progress is in `Partida.guerra` and is not saved.
- On a server: with shared police (`sv_policia_comun()`) there are no roaming patrols, no helicopter and no surrender; on any server there are no ambulances, no dropped weapons and no neighborhood war. `servidor/` has not been touched.
- When retrying a mission, `Misiones._rebobinar_jugador` returns the player to how they were but leaves the garages as they are: vehicles do not go back.
- Journeys: `Partida.conexion_hacia` first looks for a road-only route when you are at the wheel (before, the bus won if it was shorter in hops); `lugar_al_alcance` is the place that Enter opens (at the wheel, the GPS destination or an exit before another closer place) and `pista_de_llegada` the sentence on what to do at an exit, station or pier.
- `mundo.Lugar.nombre` and `.desc` are properties: with `nombre_conquistado` or `desc_conquistado` in the data, they return that text when `Partida.barrio_cambiado(barrio)` says the neighborhood no longer belongs to its standard gang. The game session registers itself in `Lugar.cambiado` when it is created. `Mundo._separar` prevents two places from sharing a point.
- `juego/escolta.py` (the `Escolta` mixin, option `escolta_misiones`: no, moderate or strong; section `escolta_misiones`): `Misiones._generar_enemigos` asks for `escolta_ajuste`, distributes the extra enemies with `escolta_grupos` and raises health and aim with `escolta_endurecer`.
- `juego/destinos.py` (the `Destinos` mixin, option `ayuda_persecucion`, section `persecuciones`): the O key (`decir_objetivo`, with `objetivo_donde` for the position), Shift O (`enfocar_objetivo`), Shift G and Control G (`quitar_gps_jugador`, `recuperar_gps`) and the chase numbers (`pers`). The destination model is in `Partida.fijar_gps`: a single destination, `self.gps`, and underneath it the one waiting (`anterior`); `quitar_gps_auto` removes the mission's or job's destination without touching what the player has set, and `_gps_cosa_acabada` closes the detour to a vehicle, a person or an item.
- Retrying a mission (`Misiones.fallar_mision`): `_rebobinar_jugador` does not roll back garages or boats; `MisVehiculos.preparar_traslado` and `llevarte_a` take the vehicle you were driving along with you.
- `Guerra.buscar_pelea(banda, larga)` reuses `Partida.guerra` with `tipo == "pelea"`: one or two rounds, with no neighborhood at stake and without requiring the `guerra_calle` mode.
- Self-defense: `actualizar_personas` marks as an aggressor anyone coming at you within 32 meters, without waiting for the first blow (`Calle.marcar_agresor`).
- Updater: `actualizador.error_de_disco` turns a Windows write error into a sentence (`ErrorDisco`), `copias_del_juego` counts how many times it is open, and `menus_actualizar.a_mano` are the steps to update without it.

### Reconquering neighborhoods

`juego/reconquista.py` (the `Reconquista` mixin of `Partida`). Offline only: `rec_nivel()` returns empty with the `reconquista` option set to `apagado`, on a server (`en_servidor`) or with a game with friends in progress (`online_activo()`), and then nothing advances.

- State: everything in `Jugador.reconquista` (a flat dictionary, saved with the game): `fase`, `calma`, `descanso`, `informantes`, `bandas` (strength, morale, defeats, `fuera`, `tregua`, `golpe`, `umbral`, `forastera`), `barrios` (loyalty, guard, reinforcement, `pisado`, `de`), `asalto` and `partes`. An older saved game loads with `{}`; `rec()` fills it on first use and `cargar_dict` sets `_rec_cargada` if it already had enough neighborhoods (initial calm and a notice, once).
- Phase (`rec_fase`): `suave` with `suave_barrios` or more, `plena` with a whole city, `mundo` with all of them. `reconquista_revisar()` is called every two seconds and every midnight.
- Every midnight (`dia_reconquista`, from `_cobrar_negocios`): payments to guards and informants, loyalty, truces, growth (`_rec_crecer`), outsider gang, and a notice of the pending assault or the plan for a new one (`_rec_planear`, `rec_elegir_objetivo`). With the reconquest active (`rec_activa()`), `sistemas.sucesos_territorio` does not launch its one-off attacks: there are never two at once.
- The assault (`asalto`): `prepara` until its day and hour; `en_curso` with `quedan` in game minutes, which goes down in `actualizar_reconquista` except during a mission or a fight. `rec_pasar_horas` (from `pasar_horas`) applies the grace period once. Defending in person uses `guerra.empezar_guerra(..., rec=...)`, which does not depend on the `guerra_calle` option; `_guerra_ganada` calls `rec_defendido("persona")`. `rec_enviar_gente` and `_rec_vence` resolve it with `_rec_calculo`.
- The truce is in `juego/pactos.py` (`oferta_tregua`, `pactar_tregua`, `menu_tregua`), with its numbers in the `pactos` section. The preemptive strike (`empezar_golpe`) is a generated repeatable mission, with four variants.
- Menus: `menu_reconquista` and `opciones_territorios` (what is added to Territories). Numbers and lines: `data/datos/vida.json`, section `reconquista` (`reconquista.AJ`); editor schema, `ajuste_reconquista`.
- Sounds: `amenaza_barrio`, `barrio_perdido`, `barrio_defendido`.
- Tests: `pruebas/test_171_reconquista.py`, which includes sixty-day simulations at each intensity.

### Life, reputation and consequences (1.7.1)

`juego/honrado.py` (the `Honrado` mixin of `Partida`) and `juego/menus_honrado.py`. It relies on the two meters that already existed, `Jugador.ciudadania` (prestige) and `Jugador.reputacion` (fame), which are independent; there is no third one.

- Level: `hon_nivel()` returns 0 to 3 according to the `consecuencias` option (`apagado`, `suave`, `normal`, `exigente`; `suave` by default) and always 0 on a server (`en_servidor`). `hon_f()` is the penalty factor (`factor_suave`, `factor_exigente`). Advantages are not scaled. The other options: `impuestos_facturas`, `antecedentes`, `pareja_familiar`.
- State, all in the player and saved with the game: `antecedentes` (list), `contrato`, `titulos`, `empresa` (per business), `creditos` (list) and `honra` (collections, debt, credit, seizure, the rumor `sospecha`, unexplained money `negro`, pending `asuntos`, lawyer, letters, bans). `hon()` creates it the first time with the grace period; an older saved game carries `_hon_cargada` and gets a one-time notice.
- Hooks: `azar.mercado` (treatment in shops, `hon_trato`), `partida.pagar` (credit, `hon_fiar`, with `_local` set in `entrar_interior`), `combate.delito` (`hon_delito`: covering up, benefit of the doubt, reporting, on file, rumor), `combate.detener` and `_trato_policia` (`hon_antecedente`), `vida.jornada` and `vida.servicio_hecho` (`hon_turno`), `partida._cobrar_negocios` (`ingreso_negocio`, `dia_honrado`), `extras3.repartir_ingresos_negocios` (period takings and seizure), `misiones` (`cumple_vida`, `hon_mision_completada`, `prestigio` in decisions), `partida.interactuar` (`hon_ofrecer_mision`: the straight-path mission goes as the last option of the place's menu), `agente.ingreso_motivo`, and `hon_negro` in robberies, tributes and repeatable jobs.
- Every midnight (`dia_honrado`): criminal records that expire, the contract evaluation, businesses (name, front, inspection), collection of bills and taxes, fees, rumor and press, extortion and unanswered matters. Every twenty seconds (`actualizar_honrado`): the notice for an old saved game, the one about the two paths, the checkpoints and the street situations.
- Pending matters (`honra["asuntos"]`: inspection, press, extortion) are resolved with `hon_resolver(asunto, clave)`; once the deadline passes they resolve themselves.
- The storyline: `data/datos/misiones_honrada.json` (chapter 16), missions with `honrada`, `requiere_vida`, `epilogos`. Its scenes have recorded voices like the others (in `data/voces/historia_principal`). The ending is stored in `decisiones["_final_honrado"]`, separate from the main story's.
- A relationship suitable for everyone: `vida_sentimental()` replaces `modo_adulto()` in relationships (`adultos.dia_adultos`, `convivencia`, `sistemas.personas_aqui`, `menus_adultos.vida_social`); the engagement and the wedding go in the relationship (`compromiso`).
- Numbers and lines: `data/datos/vida.json`, sections `vida_honrada` (`honrado.AJ`), `titulos`, `empleos_fijos`, `mejoras_negocio`, `incidencias_trabajo` and `buenas_acciones`; editor schema in `editor/esquema_honrado.py`.
- Sounds: `despido`, `inspeccion`, `factura` and `titulo` (`VERSION_CACHE` 24).
- Tests: `pruebas/test_171_honrado.py`, with the entire campaign without crimes and three sixty-day simulations (honest, criminal and double life).
- Server: none of this reaches the server yet; the player's new fields and the new income and expenses are described in the release notes for when it is brought over.

### Shopping, the pantry and cooking

- `juego/compra.py` (the `Compra` mixin, and the `Tienda` screen) and `juego/menus_cocina.py` (the menus and the `Cocinando` screen). Five sections of `vida.json`: `pasillos`, `productos`, `tipos_tienda` and `recetas` (lists) and `cocina` (the `Ajustes`). The standard content is also in the module (`PASILLOS`, `PRODUCTOS`, `TIPOS_TIENDA`, `RECETAS`), in case it is missing from older data; there is a test that checks they match.
- The shop: `tipo_tienda(l)` decides a shop's class (the place's `surtido` field, the type's `lugares` list, the `palabras` of the name, or the type with `resto`) and `surtido(l)` gives its aisles with their products. `precio_producto(l, prod)` applies the day's swing (`mercado`), the type's `recargo` and the special offer. `oferta_de(l)` and `agotado(l, prod)` come from the game's seed, the day and the shop: they are the same all day. `sistemas.con_recorrido` opens `compra.Tienda` (a child of `sistemas.Local`) in shops with a range of goods; the basket lives in that screen and is not saved. `comprar_productos(l, cosas)` charges and `robar_productos(l, cosas)` is shoplifting (`delito`, and `Jugador.locales_vetados` as in robberies).
- What you buy goes to `Jugador.encima` and, when you put it away (`guardar_compra(casa)`), to `Jugador.despensa[casa.id]`. Both are lists of batches: `{"id": producto, "n": cantidad, "cad": día en que caduca, o 0}`; a cooked dish is `{"plato": receta, "nombre", "n", "cad", "hambre", "sed", "salud", "animo"}`, with its effects already calculated, so it still works even if the recipe's mod is removed. Fridge or pantry is decided by the product's `sitio` (`lote_info`); the capacity, by `sitio_capacidad(casa, sitio)`. `tirar_caducado(lotes)` is called on opening; a batch is good up to and including day `cad`.
- Cooking: `receta_falta(casa, r)` and `cocinar(casa, r, rng)`, which uses up ingredients, passes the time, draws the quality (`Jugador.cocina_practica`, from 0 to 100, and the `cocina` furniture), serves one portion and stores the rest. `sistemas.Interior` calls `menus_cocina.usar_cocina`, which, with nothing stored and no food on you, does what it did before (`Interior._picar_algo`).
- Three new player fields (`despensa`, `encima`, `cocina_practica`), all with a standard value: the saved game format does not go up. There are no new options. It depends only on the player, so on a server it works the same (the clock does not move forward when cooking) and `servidor/` has not been touched; the world fingerprint does not change.
- New sounds: `cesta`, `nevera`, `cocina_cortar`, `cocina_freir`, `cocina_hervir` and `plato_servido` (`VERSION_CACHE` 20). In the packs they are CC0 recordings from bigsoundbank.com (see "Third-party sounds").

### The controller: nothing keyboard-only (1.7.1)

- `juego/mando.py` translates the controller into keys. Whatever does not fit on a button or a combination goes into `MENU_ACCIONES` (sentence, key, modifier, where it applies, condition, explanation, whether the menu stays open): it is the menu opened by the `menu_acciones` action, by default on the combine button plus the right trigger, on foot and at the wheel. `acciones_de(p)` gives the ones that apply at that moment.
- Rule: a new key in `ayuda.TECLAS` needs a button or combination in `POR_DEFECTO`, an entry in `MENU_ACCIONES`, or a line in `SIN_BOTON` with its reason (`SOLO_A_PIE` for general ones that make no sense at the wheel). On screens that are not menus (`PANTALLA`), a key of their own needs an alias of one of the keys the controller sends there: the C of the basket is Tab (`ALIAS_PANTALLA`). This is watched by `pruebas/test_171_mando.py`.
- Controller events carry `mando = True` (`_Ev`): they do not go through the keyboard key remapping (`App.evento_tecla`), and in `menus.EntradaTexto` they move the letter wheel. `EntradaTexto(..., propuestas=lista o función)` offers already-written texts with Tab (X or Square): the lists `frases_chat`, `rol_frases_ic`, `rol_frases_yo` and `rol_frases_ooc` in `gente.json`, and `nombres_propuestos` in `vehiculos_propios` in `vida.json`.
- Spoken sentences: `textos_mando` swaps keys for buttons also when the function is on a combination. For screens that speak with `app.decir`, `Mando.boton_pantalla(tecla, nombre)` gives the name of the key or that of the button.

### Tests

`probar.bat` runs the whole suite. The tests drive the game like a person, with keys and frames: they play every mission, travel the maps with the GPS, go into every menu and place, play twenty days with time sped up, connect two games to each other, operate a simulated controller and check the editor's accessibility. They also fail if a sound is added without a description or a key without documentation. They use neither the network nor the sound card.

### Building

`compilar.bat` uses cx_Freeze and leaves in `dist\PuertoBravo` a portable folder with `PuertoBravo.exe` and `EditorMods.exe`. Beforehand it regenerates the built-in mods, any missing sounds and the guides. When it finishes:

- It protects the data: each JSON file is compressed, encrypted and signed, and changes extension (`.pbd` data, `.pbm` mods, `.pbs` saved games, `.pbc` options). The code reads both forms, so in the source code everything stays as readable JSON.
- It checks by itself that the folder is complete, protected and clean: no saved games, options, logs, personal music or development documentation.

The protection uses a key that is inside the program itself. It prevents casual editing; it is not a barrier against someone who analyzes the executable.

### Publishing a version

The game updates by reading a `version.json` file signed with Ed25519. The source (the server, GitHub, both or a custom address) only decides where it is downloaded from: whatever is installed must be signed with the publisher's private key, whose public key is in `juego/actualizador.py`.

1. Bump `VERSION` in `juego/__init__.py` and add the release notes at the top of `data/docs/novedades_es.html` and of `data/docs/novedades_en.html`.
2. Run `compilar.bat`.
3. Run `publicar.bat`. It creates in `dist\publicar` the file `PuertoBravo_v<versión>.zip` and its signed `version.json`, and copies them to the folder written in `publicar_destino.txt`, which is the server's web folder.
4. For GitHub, create the release `v<versión>` and upload those same two files, with `notas_github.md` as the notes: `publicar.bat` leaves it in `dist\publicar`, with what's new in the version in every language that has its `novedades_<language>.html`, and prints the `gh release create` command that does it. The `version.json` also carries the release notes per language (`novedades_por_idioma`): every game since 1.7.3 reads its own, or the English ones, and earlier games keep reading `novedades`, in Spanish.

The key is created only once with `.venv\Scripts\python.exe herramientas\publicar.py clave` and is kept in `herramientas\claves`. That folder and `publicar_destino.txt` are in `.gitignore`: the private key must never be in the repository or on the server. Keep a copy off the computer. If you fork the project and want to publish your own versions, create your key and change, in `juego/actualizador.py`, the public key, the server address and the repository.

Signature verification is implemented with the standard library, following RFC 8032, and is checked in the tests with its official vectors. If the `cryptography` package is installed, that one is used.

### Old processors: the two numpys

The official numpy (2.4 onwards) requires a processor with the x86-64-v2 instructions (SSE4.2, SSSE3, POPCNT and the rest). On an older one, loading it makes Windows close the program with code 0xC000001D. For Python 3.15 there are no numpy wheels from before that requirement, so the built game ships with two:

- `lib\numpy`: the official one from PyPI, for current processors.
- `lib_antiguo\numpy`: the same version, built without requirements with `-Dcpu-baseline=none` and without BLAS. It still uses the modern instructions when the processor has them, because it picks them at startup.

`juego/procesador.py` asks Windows which instructions are available (`IsProcessorFeaturePresent`) before importing numpy and puts the right folder first. If the chosen one does not load, it tries the other; if neither loads, the game says so aloud and closes normally. The environment variable `PUERTOBRAVO_NUMPY=antigua` (or `oficial`) forces one, for testing.

The wheel for old processors is in `terceros/numpy_antiguo`, ready to use: `compilar.bat` places it in the build with `herramientas/poner_numpy_antiguo.py`. It only needs to be rebuilt if the numpy or Python version changes, with `herramientas\compilar_numpy_antiguo.bat`, which needs Visual Studio with the C++ tools. numpy is under the three-clause BSD license.

### The two sound engines

`juego/audio.py` is the classic mixer: it mixes in Python with numpy, with stereo panning, interaural delay and distance attenuation. `juego/audio_al.py` inherits all the logic from it and only changes the output: it keeps one OpenAL Soft source for each sound and lets the library do the mixing in binaural (HRTF). It is called through `ctypes`, with no Python packages, so it does not depend on the Python version. `audio_al.crear()` decides which one is used: the surround one only if the player has chosen it, the library is present and the processor is modern; in any other case, or if it fails at startup, the classic one.

The library, `soft_oal.dll` (OpenAL Soft 1.25.2, LGPL), is in `terceros/openal_soft` with its license, and `compilar.bat` copies it to `lib`. `herramientas/probar_motor_envolvente.py` runs a real test with the sound device.

### The update installer leaves a trail

`juego/actualizador.py` writes a `.bat` that replaces the program once the game has closed. That `.bat` records each step in `data/logs/actualizacion.log`: `PASO nombre`, `RESULTADO nombre codigo=N` (robocopy's code; 8 or more is a failure), `FALLO paso=... codigo=... motivo=...` and `TERMINADA bien` or `TERMINADA mal`. Robocopy writes into that same file, with `/log+`, only the errors, with the file and the Windows error. `leer_registro_instalador()` interprets it and `motivo_del_fallo()` turns it into a sentence for the player; on reopening with `--actualizacion-fallida`, the game says it and keeps it for Options, Updates. `limpiar_registro_instalador()` reduces each path to the file name, so the log can be sent.

Before closing, `problemas_para_instalar()` checks that the folder can be written to, that no other program of the game is open (by the process list and by trying to open each `.exe` for writing; an online server with its record does not count: it is stopped and started again by itself, as the server section explains) and that there is room: `espacio_para_instalar()` adds up the backup, how much each file grows and a margin. Before downloading, `problema_para_descargar()` does the sum with the ZIP, the extracted version and the copy. The tests (`pruebas/test_171_red_seguridad.py`) run the real `.bat` on dummy folders, including with a locked file; none of them go out to the internet.

### The voice engine, with a safety net

The engine in `juego/vozgen.py` is native code: if the processor lacks what it needs (AVX2, FMA and F16C), it can close the process without raising a Python error. That is why `disponible()` first checks the three capabilities (`capacidades()`: AVX2 is asked of Windows; FMA and F16C, of numpy) and, the first time, tests the engine in a separate process: the game itself launched with `--probar-motor-voz`, which `main.py` handles before opening anything and which ends with 0, 10 (does not load) or 11 (does not generate). Built with cx_Freeze it is `PuertoBravo.exe --probar-motor-voz`; from the code, `python main.py --probar-motor-voz`. The result is saved in `data/voces_generadas/motor_probado.json` with the game version and a fingerprint of the engine files, and the test is repeated if either of them changes. The test runs in the background: while it lasts, `disponible()` answers no and `cuando_se_sepa()` gives notice when it finishes. In addition, every time the game loads the engine within its process it records it, and deletes the record once it has generated the first line: if it closes halfway twice in a row, it stops trying. `parar_prueba()` kills the test process when the game closes and before updating.

### Session log

`juego/registro.py` creates in `data/logs` one folder per session, with a maximum of five. Inside are `sistema.txt` (version, Windows, screen reader, sound, mods, options), `sesion.log` (screens, keys, everything the game says, a snapshot of the game state every fifteen seconds, network, updates, slow frames and warnings), `errores.log` (each error with its traceback, the game state and the last keys and sounds) and `fallo_grave.log` if the process crashes suddenly. It also captures unhandled exceptions from the main thread and from the other threads.

Everything goes through a filter before being written: user paths, the user and computer names, IP and internet addresses, emails, keys and the names of network players are replaced. Neither what is typed in text boxes nor chat messages are ever logged. There is a test that plays a session with decoy data and fails if any of it appears in the folder. When adding anything that handles player data, use `registro.privado()` or `registro.ocultar()`.

### Accessibility

Accessibility is the project's main requirement. Everything that happens in the game has its own sound, and all text goes through the screen reader. All the keys are in the help (`F1`) and in the manual, which are generated from the same code. The editor follows accessibility guidelines for wxPython: labels associated with each control, access keys without repeats, native controls and context help with `Control+H`.

## 3. Creating mods

A mod adds or changes content without touching the original data: places, missions with all their steps, weapons, vehicles, races, food, items, fish, playable characters, chapters, entire maps and, for adult mode, people, plans, gifts and substances. Mods are in `data\mods` and are loaded at startup. A mission or a step can depend on several decisions at once with `si_cruce` (`"concejal:1, trono:3"`), `si_cruce_min` and `si_cruce_max`, and a mission can require one from each branch with `requiere_alguna` (`"fd2 fd3 fd4; vd2 vd3"`). Each map says where the train line runs with `tren_eje` and `tren_n`, and a place of type `aeropuerto` with `"aerodromo": true` is a town's short airstrip. In any text, whatever changes depending on whether the protagonist is a man or a woman is written in square brackets, with the masculine form, a slash and the feminine form: `Bienvenid[o|a]`, `[el sobrino|la sobrina]`. It is resolved by `juego/genero.py` just before the text is spoken.

### With the editor

The usual way is `EditorMods.exe` (or `editor.bat` from the source code). It is an application accessible with a screen reader: each thing is created by filling in a form, `Control+H` explains the field you are on and `F1` opens its manual (`data\docs\editor_mods_en.html`). Before saving, it checks that everything the mod refers to exists, and warns about anything missing.

The editor always saves in protected form, as `.pbm`, which is what the built game loads, and it opens both `.pbm` and `.json` files.

### By hand

A mod is also a JSON file. The format is explained, with examples, in `desarrollo/formato_de_los_mods.html`, and `desarrollo/ejemplo_mod.json` is a minimal mod. From the source code, the game loads the `.json` files in `data\mods` directly. The built game does not: it warns at startup and asks for the file to be opened with the editor and saved, so that it is checked and protected.

### Built-in mods

Eight come with the game, already enabled, and they also serve as models. They are generated with `herramientas/crear_mods_ejemplo.py`, where you can read how each one is made:

- Bahía del Ocaso: a large coastal city, with six neighborhoods, three gangs and a story of twenty-one missions (`herramientas/mod_bahia_ocaso.py`).
- Costa Dorada: a fishing town with its own story and an example of everything a mod can hold.
- Valdeolmos: a wine-growing town, with a wine fraud and a tractor race.
- Peñas Altas: a mountain mining town, with a mine that someone exploits at night.
- Isla de los Vientos: an island you reach by sea, with wreckers and salt flats.
- Noches de neón: Puerto Bravo by night, with no new map, with night taxis and social life.
- Oficios y recados: more honest life in Puerto Bravo.
- Motor y asfalto: cars, a motorbike, races and missions at the wheel.

To share a mod, pass on its `.pbm` file. Whoever receives it copies it into their `data\mods`.

Missions can have recorded voices (`juego/voces.py`): sound files in `data\voces`, one per line. Lines that depend on the protagonist have several forms, with an at sign after the name: `m01_1_1@alex`, `m01_1_1@m`, `m01_1_2@alex_m`. The editor writes the script with the name of each file and its forms. Mods with material from other games or voices cloned from real people are not accepted.

The voices that come with the game are generated with Phonon 40m, a voice model by [Gradium](https://gradium.ai/phonon) under a Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial license (CC BY-NC 4.0). The same model ships with the game, in `data\phonon`, to generate on the player's computer the lines that name a character they created (`juego/vozgen.py`), with the `ptts` package, which is free software. The license text is in `data\phonon\LICENCIA.txt`. The model file, `model.q8.gguf`, weighs 95 megabytes and is not in this repository: `herramientas\preparar_phonon.py` copies it from a folder with the model downloaded from Hugging Face (`gradium/phonon-40m-multilingual`), and `compilar.bat` calls it. Without the model the game works just the same; those lines are spoken by the screen reader.

## 4. Sound and music packs

The complete guide, with the description and duration of each of the 279 sounds, is `data\docs\cambiar_sonidos_en.html` (in Spanish, `cambiar_sonidos_es.html`). In short:

### Sounds

- All the effects are in `data\sonidos`, in subfolders. `por_defecto` holds the standard ones, one file per sound; if you delete one, the game creates it again. Each of the other subfolders is a pack.
- The pack is chosen inside the game, in Options, Sound and music, and you hear it right away.
- Four complete packs are included: Realista and Alternativo, with real public domain recordings; Retro, with old-console sound; and Envolvente, for the surround sound engine. All four keep the original for the orientation sounds (beacons, tones, traffic lights, radar and the sounds of places), because getting around the game depends on them.
- To make your own, create a folder in `data\sonidos` and copy your recordings into it with the exact name of the sound they replace: `claxon.wav`, `paso_acera0.ogg`. The game's sounds are in FLAC, lossless; yours can be WAV, OGG, FLAC or MP3. You do not need to bring them all: whatever is missing plays the original. WAV, OGG, FLAC and MP3 are supported.
- An optional `pack.json` file gives the pack its name, author, license and per-sound volume adjustments.
- Volume is evened out automatically: each changed sound is brought to the level of the original, and the player can raise or lower any of them separately from Options.

### Music

- Radio: each station has its file in `data\radio`. A station can have several songs (`bravo.mp3`, `bravo_1.mp3`, `bravo_2.mp3`...), which rotate. Any other audio file you leave there is a new station, and `data\radio\mi_musica` is the station with your own music. Songs longer than five minutes are read and played in one-minute chunks (`juego/musica.py`), so a recording several hours long takes up no more memory than a short song.
- Ambience: `data\musica` has one folder per situation (`menu`, `exploracion`, `noche`, `mar` and `tension`). All the pieces in each one are used, at random, whatever they are called.
- All the music is leveled automatically when it is loaded, so no track sounds louder than another.

### Third-party sounds

The Realista and Alternativo packs are put together with public domain recordings (CC0 license) from kenney.nl, opengameart.org, freesound.org and bigsoundbank.com. Each pack has a `CREDITOS.txt` file with the origin of each sound.

Pack rules, with their watchdog tests in `pruebas/test_171_sonidos.py`:

- The five folders in `data/sonidos` (`por_defecto`, `realista`, `alternativo`, `retro` and `envolvente`) have exactly the same sounds, with the same names and none in two formats. A new sound is added to `sintesis.generar()` (at the end, bumping `VERSION_CACHE`), to `catalogo_sonidos` (group and description) and to the packs with `herramientas/crear_packs.py anadir <nombres>`.
- `crear_packs.py anadir` is also used to change a sound already installed: it puts in the recording given by the pack's table (`PACKS`, `EXTRA`, `CALLE`, `V171`, `PROPIAS`) or, if there is none, the standard sound; the Retro pack inherits from Realista whatever has no console sound, and Envolvente copies from Realista whatever is not orientation or one of its own ambiences. It then rewrites `CREDITOS.txt` (one line per recording, the list of those that are the standard sound and the count in the header) and the counts in `pack.json`, so they always match the files.
- Individual recordings carry their title, author and page in `DETALLE`, and that is how they appear in `CREDITOS.txt`. Those from 1.7.1 are from bigsoundbank.com, all CC0 and by Joseph SARDIN except where noted: megaphone ("Megaphone, larsen" 9 and 4), radio set ("Walkie talkie: Start" and "Keypad tones"), doorbell ("Doorbell house #1"; "Doorbell #6", by Dorian CLAIR), fridge ("Fridge door"), kitchen ("Cutting board, zucchini" 2 and 1, "Frying pan" 3 and 2, "Boiling water", "Small Broth in a Pot"), plate ("Large Plate, set on Table", "Small plate, set on table"), basket ("Pack of chips, manipulation #2"; "Tin can #1", by DenisChardonnet) and neighborhoods ("Bass Tom, #1", repeated three times; "Church Bell"; "Drum roll 3 S" and "Drum Roll 1 L"). The downloaded originals are kept outside the repository, in `adjuntos/fuentes/bigsoundbank_171`, with their `LEEME.txt`.
- Trimming options in `crear_packs.py`: besides `dur`, `golpe`, `bucle` and `desde`, it accepts `entrada` and `fundido` (seconds of fade at the start and at the end, for continuous noises that get cut off) and `veces` (the same take repeated at those moments).
- `chat_personaje`, `chat_fuera`, `chat_accion` and `espera_servidor` are the standard sound in every pack: they are a code of tones, not the noise of a thing.
- The orientation sounds in the packs are those in `por_defecto`, byte for byte where it matters: the test compares the samples.
- There is a single classification of sounds, in `juego/catalogo_sonidos.py`: `GRUPOS` (title, tip for the guide and key, in the order in which they are taught) and `SONIDOS` (for each group, file, spoken name and explanation of what hearing it means). From there come, with the same groups, names and order, Learn the sounds (`menus_juego.sonidos`), Options, Adjust the volume of each sound (`menus_juego.volumen_sonidos`), the sounds guide and the list for packs (`generar_datos.py`); all of them go through `catalogo_sonidos.clasificar`, which sorts each group alphabetically by the spoken name (`orden`: without accents and with ñ after n). There are 20 groups, by what is done in the game: orientation and GPS; sounds of places; moving on foot and swimming; vehicles and driving; breakdowns, repair shop, gas and vehicle theft; boats, helicopters, train, tram and plane; weapons, the shot of each one; combat, aim and wounds; police and emergency services; people on the street and animals; home, kitchen and food; stores, money and items; menus, phone and game alerts; missions, work, reputation and mood; territories and gangs; online play and roleplay; fishing, casino and games; ambience and weather; relationships, only with adult content; substances and dealing, only with adult content. None may fall into "Other sounds", which only exists for a file added by hand.
- `IMPORTANTES` is the order of the group "The most important", and the glossary `ayuda.SONIDOS` is derived from it. `es_orientacion` says which sounds the packs keep (it does not depend on the groups). `buscar` is the search used by both menus (`menus_juego.buscar_sonido`): all the words, in the name, explanation, group or file name, ignoring accents and capitals.
- The per-sound volume is saved in `vol_sonidos` by file name, so reclassifying loses nothing. `packs.factor`, `packs.cambiado` and `packs.restablecer` give the adjustment that is actually applied, say whether it has been changed and return it to the standard value; the menu (`MenuVolumenes`) adds Delete, the "Changed sounds" list and resetting a group.
