Changing the sounds and music of Puerto Bravo

All the game's sounds are ordinary files in the data folder, next to the program. You can replace any of them with a recording of your own. This document explains how, and describes every file. It is organised by headings: with your screen reader you can jump from one to the next with the H key.

1. Where everything is

  • data\sonidos: all the sound effects, spread over subfolders.
  • data\sonidos\por_defecto: the standard sounds, one file per sound. There are 283 files. If one is missing or you delete it, the game creates it again.
  • data\radio: the music of the radio stations, one file per station.
  • data\radio\mi_musica: your own station. Copy here the songs you want.
  • data\musica: the ambient music, with one folder per situation and several pieces in each.
  • data\sonidos\realista, data\sonidos\alternativo, data\sonidos\retro and the other subfolders: the sound packs, one per folder. You choose them in Options.

2. How to change a sound effect

  1. Find in the list at the end the name of the sound you want to change, for example claxon.
  2. Prepare your recording and give it exactly that name: claxon.wav.
  3. Copy it into data\sonidos\por_defecto. You don't need to delete the game's file, which is called claxon.flac: if there are two with the same name, yours is the one that plays.
  4. Close the game and open it again. Sounds are loaded at startup.

Supported formats

  • WAV, OGG, FLAC and MP3. The sounds that come with the game are FLAC. If there are two files with the same name and a different extension, the one that is not FLAC is used, which is yours. To go back to the game's sound, delete yours.
  • Why FLAC: it compresses without loss. Reading it gives exactly the same samples as a WAV, but it takes less than half the space. Up to version 1.6.1 the game's sounds were WAV; when updating, the game deletes the old WAV files that are identical to the new ones and keeps the ones you have changed. OGG and MP3 take even less space, but lose some quality: they are fine for your recordings if you don't mind, and the game only uses them where the original already came that way, which is the music, the radio, the voices and the long ambiences of the Surround pack.
  • Any sample rate: the game converts it to 44100 hertz.
  • Mono or stereo. Effects are always turned into mono, because it is the game that places each sound to your left, to your right, in front or behind.

Getting an original sound back

Delete the file. The next time it starts, the game will create the original sound again. To get them all back, delete the whole data\sonidos\por_defecto folder.

Tips so it sounds right

  • Trim the silence at the start: if the sound takes a moment to begin, the game will seem to respond late.
  • Keep a length similar to the original's, above all for footsteps, alerts and beacons. The list gives the length of each one.
  • Don't leave it at full volume. The originals peak between 30 and 90 percent depending on how important they are. If yours sounds too loud or too soft, adjust it in your editor.
  • Avoid long reverb on orientation sounds: it blurs the direction they come from.
  • Sounds marked as a loop repeat without stopping. The end must join the start with no click or jump: cut at a zero crossing or use a crossfade.
  • Engines are recorded at idle and at a steady speed. The game raises the pitch as you accelerate, up to a little more than double.

The volume evens out on its own

Every recording comes at a different volume. So that this doesn't spoil the game's balance, when it loads a sound you have changed the game brings it to the same level the original had: it measures the loudest stretch of your file, compares it with the original sound's and raises or lowers it as needed, without letting it clip. That way some footsteps can't end up louder than a gunshot, or a beacon inaudible. You can turn this off in Options, Sound and music, Even out the volume of the changed sounds.

If a particular sound still seems loud or soft to you, go to Options, Sound and music, Adjust the volume of each sound. They are all there, in the same groups as the list at the end of this document: Enter plays it and the left and right arrows lower or raise it, from 30 to 200 percent. Search for a sound finds it by a word of its name, without having to know which group it is in. The menu says how many sounds you have changed, in total and in each group, and lets you return a single one, a whole group or all of them to their standard volume. The setting is saved with your options and goes by the file name, so it isn't lost when you update the game.

Listening to any sound inside the game

In the main menu and the pause menu, Learn the sounds has the game's 283 sounds: first the group The most important, then Search for a sound and then all of them, in the same groups as the list at the end of this document and in the same order. Go into a group and press Enter on a sound: the game says what it is called and what it means, and plays it on its own, with the pack you have chosen. With Search for a sound you type a word, for example siren, and you get all the ones that have it in their name or in their explanation. It is useful to check how one of your recordings turned out without having to trigger the sound while playing.

3. Sound packs

A pack is a folder with files named the same as the game's sounds. Packs go inside data\sonidos, each in its own subfolder, next to por_defecto, and you choose them inside the game in Options, Sound and music, Sound pack, with the left and right arrows. The change is heard at once and doesn't touch your files in data\sonidos\por_defecto: to go back, choose «default».

  • A pack of yours doesn't have to bring them all: the sounds it doesn't bring keep playing with the original.
  • The volume of each sound in the pack is evened out to the original's, as explained above.
  • The packs that come with the game bring exactly the same files as por_defecto, with the same names: not one more and not one less. Their recordings are public domain (CC0 licence) and come from kenney.nl, opengameart.org, freesound.org and bigsoundbank.com.
  • The tones of the role-play channels (chat_personaje, chat_fuera and chat_accion) and the one for waiting for the server (espera_servidor) are the standard ones in every pack, on purpose: they are a code of tones and it helps if they always sound the same.
  • The packs installed as standard are «Alternative», with 283 sounds, «Surround», with 283 sounds, «Realistic», with 283 sounds, «Retro», with 283 sounds. Each one brings all the game's sounds: they replace with public domain recordings everything that is not orientation, and keep the original for the orientation sounds: beacons, tones, traffic lights, radar, locators and the sounds of places. Each pack's CREDITOS.txt file says where each sound comes from and which ones are original.

Making your own pack

  1. Create a folder inside data\sonidos with the name of the pack, with no odd spaces: for example mi_pack.
  2. Copy your recordings into it with the exact name of the sound they replace: claxon.wav, paso_acera0.ogg. The list at the end of this document has every name.
  3. Open the game and choose the pack in Options.

If you like, add a pack.json file with the name that will be read out in Options and a few settings:

{
  "nombre": "Mi pack",
  "descripcion": "Grabado en mi calle",
  "autor": "Tu nombre",
  "licencia": "CC0",
  "igualar": true,
  "volumenes": {"claxon": 0.8, "ladrido": 1.3}
}
  • igualar: with false, this pack's sounds come in as they are, without evening out the volume. Use it only if you have already levelled them yourself.
  • volumenes: a per-sound touch-up applied after evening out. 1 leaves it as it is, 0.8 lowers it by twenty percent.

To share a pack, compress its folder. Whoever receives it unzips it inside their data\sonidos.

4. The radio

Changing the music of a station

Each standard station has a file name in data\radio. Replace the file with another song with the same name and that one will play.

A station can have several songs: every file named like the station, or like the station followed by an underscore and whatever you like, is one of its songs. For example bravo.mp3, bravo_1.mp3 and bravo_dos.ogg are three songs of Bravo FM: they play at random and, when one ends, a different one comes on. With a single song, it repeats in a loop.

  • bravo.mp3: Bravo FM, road rock.
  • latina.mp3: Onda Caribe, Latin rhythms.
  • neon.mp3: Neón 84, synthesizers.
  • calma.mp3: Radio Bahía, relaxing music.
  • hierro.mp3: Radio Hierro, hard rock from Monteferro.
  • club.mp3: Lucero Club, electronic.
  • tropical.mp3: Costa Tropical, street party music.

If you delete one of these files, the game composes the original music again. Songs are levelled automatically when loaded: it doesn't matter at what volume each file comes.

Adding new stations

Any other audio file you leave in data\radio is a new station, named after the file. For example, radio_clasica.mp3 appears on the dial as «Radio clasica». Underscores are read as spaces.

My music

The files in data\radio\mi_musica make up the «My music» station. Inside the car, the R key goes from one song to the next before jumping to the next station.

5. The ambient music

Outside vehicles, or with the radio off, soft background music plays that changes with the situation. Each situation has several pieces: the game starts with one at random and, when it ends, moves on to a different one. When you turn on a vehicle's radio the ambient music goes quiet, and it comes back when you turn the radio off or get out.

Each situation is a folder inside data\musica:

  • data\musica\exploracion: by day, exploring at ease. As standard it has 4 pieces, from exploracion_1.mp3 to exploracion_4.mp3.
  • data\musica\noche: at night. As standard it has 3 pieces, from noche_1.mp3 to noche_3.mp3.
  • data\musica\mar: at sea. As standard it has 3 pieces, from mar_1.mp3 to mar_3.mp3.
  • data\musica\tension: with the police on your tail or enemies nearby. As standard it has 3 pieces, from tension_1.mp3 to tension_3.mp3.
  • data\musica\menu: in the main menu. As standard it has 4 pieces, from menu_1.mp3 to menu_4.mp3.
  • To add your own music, copy audio files into the folder of the situation you want. Every file in the folder joins the rotation, however many there are: if you leave an exploracion_5.wav in exploracion, or twenty songs with any name, they are all used at random along with the ones already there. WAV, OGG, FLAC and MP3 are all fine.
  • All music is levelled automatically when loaded: each ambient piece, each station, each new station and each song of My music end up at the same level, so none sounds louder than another. The only thing that changes the volume is what you choose in Options.
  • To remove a standard piece so that it doesn't come back, replace it with another one with the same name. If you just delete it, the game composes it again.
  • For only your music to play in a situation, replace all the standard pieces in its folder.
  • Ambient music is turned on, turned off and has its volume set in Options, Sound and music.
